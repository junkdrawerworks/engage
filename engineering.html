Balance the warp core

One shift, eight orders from the bridge, an hour each. Feed the core antimatter and deuterium to make the power, and coolant to keep it below its rating. Antimatter with no deuterium to meet strains the containment; so does a core run too hot. Strain it to the limit and the core is ejected.

    How rich a mix?

    The Technical Manual

    The Next Generation Technical Manual (1991) runs the core rich: 25 parts deuterium to one of antimatter while it idles, 10 to 1 at warp 1, leaner as the ship goes faster, and one to one only at warp 8 and above. The extra deuterium isn’t burned; it is heated into the plasma that drives the warp coils.

    The Academy exam

    In “Coming of Age” (The Next Generation, season 1, 1988), the question on Wesley Crusher’s entrance exam has one right answer: one to one. That is the ratio at which every gram of antimatter meets a gram of matter, and nothing is left over.

    This core does both: it burns one to one, and any deuterium you add past that runs through it and carries heat away, at the cost of the tank.

    The energy is real: a kilogram of antimatter meeting a kilogram of matter turns both into energy, E = mc² for 2 kilograms, 1.8 × 1017 joules or 43 megatons of TNT. The Hiroshima bomb was about 15 kilotons and the Tsar Bomba 50 megatons; the world used just over 600 exajoules of energy in 2025 (Energy Institute, Statistical Review of World Energy 2026). The core’s size (a kilogram of antimatter an hour at full burn), the power each warp factor needs and how it heats are this game’s, not canon. The mix ratios are as fan references give the manual and the episode.
